MAIN HABITS

A habit starts with a cue

WHAT A HABIT IS

Cue

a time, place or feeling

Urge

kicks in on its own

Action

no decision needed

The cue starts it, not a decision.

A habit isn’t just something you do a lot. It’s an urge learned by repeating the same behavior in the same situation.

WHY WILLPOWER FAILS

Motivation fades

Habit keeps running

That’s why New Year’s resolutions fade. Gardner et al. (2024) call the mismatch between habit theory and real life the “theory-reality gap.”

SAME TIME, SAME PLACE

Why is brushing your teeth automatic?

Because the cue never changes: same time, same place, every single night.

SAME TIME

SAME PLACE

Habits form when the context stays the same, and bedtime is full of contexts like this.

HOW LONG IT TAKES

Weeks,

Habits build slowly with repetition. Missing one day won’t ruin your progress.

(Gardner et al., 2024)

IF-THEN PLANS

WHEN

I brush my teeth at night,

THEN

I write tomorrow’s to-do list.

“When X happens, I do Y.” Tying a new habit to a cue you already have makes it easier to stick with.

MAIN WRITING BEFORE BED

Specific to-do lists win

THE RESULT

~9 min

faster to fall asleep for the to-do list group

Most people guess listing what you already finished is the calming option. It wasn’t.

SPECIFICITY MATTERS

VAGUE · HELPS LESS

“Study”

SPECIFIC · HELPS MORE

“Read chapter 4 for bio at 3 PM”

The more specific the list, the faster people fell asleep.

SUB-THE RESULT

MAIN WRITING BEFORE BED

Unfinished tasks nag you

WHY IT WORKS

the bio lab…

that email

quiz Friday?

did I submit it?

work shift…

Masicampo & Baumeister (2011): unfinished tasks keep popping into your head and get in the way of other thinking. (Related to the Zeigarnik effect.)

THE KEY FINDING

“Consider it done.”

Making a specific plan for a task stopped those intrusive thoughts, even though the task wasn’t done yet.

Your brain treats a plan almost like progress.

SUB-WHY IT WORKS

MAIN WRITING BEFORE BED

Hand your tasks to the paper

CONNECTING THE STUDIES

Your head

rehearsing tasks all night

The paper

holds them for you

Masicampo explains why Scullin’s result happened. The list takes your open tasks, so your mind stops rehearsing them at night.

TONIGHT’S ROUTINE

5 minutes, that’s it

On paper, not your phone

Specific tasks, each with a time

Right after a bedtime cue, like brushing your teeth

A cue plus a small action: that’s topic 1 all over again.

MAIN CAFFEINE

Caffeine hides your sleep pressure

HOW CAFFEINE WORKS

Adenosine builds up all day. That’s your “sleep pressure,” the thing that makes you sleepy at night.

WAKE UP

BEDTIME

CAFFEINE BLOCKS IT

You don’t feel tired, but the pressure is still there. And caffeine stays in your system for hours, longer than most people think.

THE DRAKE STUDY (2013)

MAIN LIGHT

Light sets your body clock

WRIGHT ET AL., 2013 · A WEEK OF CAMPING

No electric lights. Only the sun.

2 hrs

earlier body clocks, lined up with sunrise and sunset

NIGHT OWLS

LOOKED LIKE EARLY BIRDS

WHAT IT MEANS

Light is the main signal that sets your body clock.

More daylight and less electric light at night moved people’s sleepiness earlier in the evening.

Your phone and room lights at midnight work against that.

SUB-A WEEK OF CAMPING

MAIN LIGHT

Even one weekend works

STOTHARD ET AL., 2017

They repeated the camping study in winter and found a similar effect. Then they tried just one weekend:
